From above, the suburb appears orderly and familiar, but compared to 1977 it is now fully embedded in global tracking systems. Every street and roofline is mapped in near real time by Earth-observation and mapping satellites, forming the basis of tools like digital navigation and location services. Mobile towers are spaced through the landscape, maintaining constant connectivity between homes, devices, and networks that didn’t exist in any comparable form in 1977. Beneath the streets, expanded energy and data infrastructure links the suburb into wider regional grids. Property data is also continuously updated and analysed, meaning each house is not just a physical structure, but part of a live economic system of pricing, ownership, and prediction. Compared to 1977, where the suburb was primarily a physical environment of homes and streets, it is now deeply intertwined with invisible layers of data, connectivity, and economic systems that shape how the space is experienced and understood.
