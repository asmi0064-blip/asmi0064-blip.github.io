Inside the café, several tables are occupied, yet each group exists in its own digital bubble. Bluetooth earbuds and connected devices silently link people to private audio streams, while Wi-Fi turns the entire space into a shared but invisible network of constant data exchange. At the counter, QR code menus replace physical interaction, shifting ordering and communication onto personal screens. Compared to 1977, where cafés were defined by conversation and shared attention, this space is now structured by invisible connectivity—where technology enables proximity, but often reinforces social isolation.
